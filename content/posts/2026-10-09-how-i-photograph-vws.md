VWs are great cars to photograph. Clean lines, good stance, and owners who love to talk about every part they've changed. Still, the difference between a snapshot and a frame someone wants on a wall comes down to a few habits. This is how I shoot them for VolksVision, whether I have a camera or just my phone.

## Start with the light

The best car photos happen when the sun is low: the first hour after sunrise and the last hour before sunset. The light is soft, the paint glows instead of glaring, and the chrome doesn't blow out. Midday Florida sun is the hardest light there is for cars. If I have to shoot then, I look for open shade.

## Angles that flatter a build

- **Front three-quarter, low.** Crouch to about headlight height and shoot the front corner. It's the classic for a reason.
- **Straight side profile** to show stance and fitment. Get level with the wheel centers.
- **Details.** Badges, wheels, the intake, the engine bay. These sell the story.
- **Turn the wheels** slightly so you see the face of the wheel, not the tread.

## Settings I use

On a camera, I start around **f/5.6 to f/8** so the whole car is sharp, keep ISO as low as the light allows, and use a shutter speed fast enough to hold steady. For detail shots I open up to **f/2.8** so the background falls away. On a phone, I tap to focus on the car, then pull the exposure down a little so the bright paint and sky keep their color.

## Clean the frame

Before I press the shutter I check the edges: trash cans, cones, other cars, my own shadow. Moving three steps fixes most backgrounds. Murals, water and older Florida buildings around St. Pete make great backdrops when the owner and property allow it.

## Edit lightly

I keep edits honest. Straighten the horizon, lift the shadows a touch, warm it slightly if the light was cool, and stop there. A build should look like itself on its best day.

## Ask first, credit always

Most owners say yes when you ask to shoot their car, and many will move it into better light for you. Offer to send them the photos. That's how a lot of the frames on this site happened.
